Most people leave the house looking like they got dressed in the dark and hoped no one would notice.

I want the other thing back. The America you meet in good books and movies, where a man puts on a shirt that fits and shoes he can walk in, and you can tell he meant to be seen.

This is not about money. You can do it with a thrifted jacket and a clean pair of leather shoes. It is about respect. For the room. For the people in it. For yourself.

Clothes are a social norm. When the norm dies, everything gets sloppier: meetings, dates, the way we stand in line. Looking like you meant to leave the house is a small way to push back.

I dress like I have somewhere to be. Even when the somewhere is a card shop.
